# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether an item gives timely, credible, decision-relevant information about US consumer demand, a tracked consumer company's trajectory (especially Lululemon and athleisure peers), or the macro forces driving US consumer spending.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Thesis-changing.** Earnings or comps results with explicit figures vs guidance, guidance changes, CEO/CFO changes at tracked companies, major market-share developments, Fed emergency moves, tariff regimes materially changing apparel costs.
- **7-8: Material data.** Retail sales and confidence prints with actual vs expected, company quarterly numbers with baselines, confirmed store-footprint or China-expansion moves by tracked brands, concrete third-party tracking data (sell-through, pricing, traffic).
- **5-6: Useful context.** Credible analyst estimates with explicit numbers, hiring/product news of tracked brands, consumer credit data, secondary retail read-throughs.
- **3-4: Low value.** Routine price-move recaps, ratings changes without new data, conference appearances, seasonal pattern commentary.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumors, promotional content, lifestyle/celebrity coverage, unexplained numbers.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward explicit comparable-sales figures, guidance ranges, and actual-vs-expected comparisons. A "beat" or "miss" deserves a score only when the item states the numbers. Distinguish company guidance from analyst estimates from reported actuals. For macro data, the surprise vs consensus matters more than the level. Competition news scores when it names the share or price mechanism, not merely "brand X is gaining popularity".

Use three to five specific topic tags, e.g. `lulu-comps`, `athleisure-competition`, `us-retail-sales`, `fed-rates`, `apparel-tariffs`.
